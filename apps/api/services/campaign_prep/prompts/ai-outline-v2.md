# AI adventure chapter outline — v1.2
Input: the accepted premise and cast, content_contract, size, size_profile and the chapter archetypes. Output: one AiOutline. Each chapter is written afterwards in its own call, in play order, from your plan.

Plan the number of chapters in size_profile.chapters. Follow the proven published shape described by size_profile.arc. Typical archetypes: hook (an exciting, focused opening, often with early action), hub (a settlement with people, rumours, services and a local threat), investigation, journey, site (an optional or required location to explore), stronghold (the antagonist's base with several ways in), climax (where the tension peaks and the outcome depends on choices) and aftermath. With three or more chapters, the final chapter is a climax, stronghold or aftermath. A medium or larger adventure should offer at least one choice of route or an optional lead.

Each chapter names a primary location from the cast's places, the cast entities it uses, and what the antagonist does meanwhile (draw on the scheme timeline so the world moves). Every shared entity appears in at least one chapter. leads_onward lists at least two independent leads that can point the heroes to the next chapter (people, evidence, places, events), so one missed clue never stalls the adventure; the final chapter has none.

Plan size_profile.combat_encounters fights in total in combat_encounters, spread where they serve the story: early action in the hook, guardians or rivals at sites, the antagonist's forces in the stronghold or climax. Each names the opponents, the stakes and how the heroes might avoid, end or win the fight without slaughter (parley, stealth, surrender, retreat, a hazard turned against the foe). Combat is one approach among several, never the only way past a goal.

Honor content_contract as well as size. If combat is requested, plan at least one executable optional combat encounter even when a Small profile permits zero. A request for combat choices or a premise promising encounters needs actual encounters, verified profiles and tactical running guidance. If combat is explicitly excluded, plan zero encounters regardless of the usual size range. An unpromised social adventure may omit encounters where its size allows; do not add a fight merely because danger or an antagonist exists.

Place every campaign secret: list it in sets_up_secret_ids of the chapters that foreshadow it and in reveals_secret_ids of the chapter or chapters where it can be revealed. Each secret is set up no later than its first reveal; secrets that reshape the story are best foreshadowed early and revealed in later chapters. Your choices are restricted to the existing cast and secret IDs.

Plan the usable format of a published adventure module: an immediate hook and stakes, a cast with motives/knowledge, coherent travel and keyed locations, clue redundancy, encounters with tactics/morale and nonviolent approaches, rewards and conditional endings. Chapters are connected situations, not a mandatory list of player actions. Preserve an actionable escalation and finale on more than one credible route, including failure, retreat and later return. Keep public geography separate from secret rooms and earned reveals. Required goals must be achievable using supported ordinary exploration, dialogue, item custody, checks and verified combat; do not make an unsupported spell or authored magic-item effect the sole essential resolution.

slug is a short readable name; the chapter ID is derived from its position and slug. brief tells the chapter's author the practical situation, its purpose and its connections. dm_guidance explains how the chapters connect and pace.

When revision is supplied, return the complete corrected outline that fixes every listed problem and keeps sound chapters unchanged.
