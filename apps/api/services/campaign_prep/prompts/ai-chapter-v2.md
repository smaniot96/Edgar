# AI adventure chapter — v1.2
Input: the accepted premise, cast and full outline, requested_chapter (its plan, ID and position), story_so_far (accepted earlier chapters: their summaries, goals, events and new identities), existing_ids, existing_combatants (actor ID -> bound creature), verified_creatures (exact names with challenge ratings), size, size_profile and preferences. Output: one AiChapterDraft for requested_chapter only. Later chapters are written after you and receive your continuity_summary.

Write size_profile.events_per_chapter events and size_profile.goals_per_chapter goals. An event is a meaningful situation at a place, not a scripted action: give it a trigger, timing, a player-safe public_description of what is perceived on arrival, and dm_notes with motives, opportunities, clues, tactics, alternative approaches and consequences. Mix exploration, social interaction, discovery and, where it serves the story, combat, as the chapter's archetype suggests. Connect events with next_event_ids inside this chapter; allow non-linear play where it fits. entry_event_id is where the heroes arrive (they come through the previous chapter's leads). exit_event_id is the scene where this chapter's leads onward converge and the heroes move on; the next chapter begins after it. Every event must be reachable from the entry, and every event must be able to reach the exit through next_event_ids (side scenes link back toward the exit), so the heroes can always move on whichever scenes they skip. In the final chapter the exit is the resolution.

Goals describe outcomes the heroes can achieve by any credible creative approach. Mark required only what the adventure truly cannot continue without; optional goals reward exploration. Every event serves at least one goal and every goal lists its events.

Continuity: reference shared cast entities and secrets by their existing IDs, and earlier chapters' identities from story_so_far. Never redeclare an existing ID; new_entities and new_secrets are only for genuinely new local people, places, factions, clues and private truths, with new IDs not in existing_ids (prefixing them with the chapter's slug keeps them distinct). Every event location_id is a place. The heroes arrive through the leads from the previous chapter; acknowledge how. Follow the plan: at least one event at primary_location_id; reference every secret in sets_up_secret_ids in an event's secret_ids and foreshadow it in dm_notes without disclosing it; reference every secret in reveals_secret_ids in an event's secret_ids and make it discoverable through a reveal or dm_notes with an earned condition. Keep consequential truths out of public descriptions until earned. Show the antagonist_pressure in the world.

Write every fight in requested_chapter.combat_encounters as an event with encounters. Each opponent group is an NPC entity used as actor_id (create a new NPC entity such as a gang or a beast pack when needed; a faction cannot fight) and a participant of that event. Players attack foes by the words the scene uses, so a fighting NPC has a short natural name of one or two words, plural for a group (for example Salvagers, Sluice Guards, Eels), used verbatim in that event's description or notes, with aliases such as the singular. Do not put places or adjectives in the name (not Rooftop Salvagers). Choose an exact verified_creatures name whose challenge suits the premise's level range and the fiction, and a sensible quantity; dm_notes and tactics say how the foes fight, when they flee or surrender, and how the heroes can avoid or end the fight. One actor is always the same creature: existing_combatants lists actors already bound to a creature, so reuse that creature or create a new actor.

Reveals expose a hidden entity (a clue, a person's true nature, a hidden place) with earned public_text and a private condition; reveal entity_ids are entities present in the event. Checks are optional fixed-DC skill, ability or tool checks (for example insight, athletics, arcana, thieves_tools), never the only solution. items give important objects (represented by clue entities) clear operating procedures.

running_notes is the chapter's practical running reference: geography, NPC voices and attitudes, timing, rewards and what happens if the heroes delay. continuity_summary tells later chapter authors what this chapter establishes, its possible outcomes and its open threads.

When revision is supplied, return the complete corrected chapter that fixes every listed problem and keeps sound content and IDs unchanged.
