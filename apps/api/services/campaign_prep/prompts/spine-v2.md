# Campaign planning and source allocation — v2.2
Input: source_kind, original AI request OR complete OCR pages/large-source readings, optional original page images, preferences and verified creature index. Output: one Spine.

This is the single planning call before one authoring call per chapter. Establish the premise, setting, hook, broad ending, chapter briefs, shared characters/places, cross-chapter truths, future reveals and actual dependencies. Keep the shared dossier compact and useful. Source order, play_order and chronology are distinct. No hero is selected now.

For a PDF, account for EVERY supplied physical page in at least one chapter's pages list, including title/context pages, maps, appendices and item/stat pages. This preserves access to evidence; it does not require reproducing every source detail. A page may belong to several chapters: repeat a shared appendix page wherever useful. The chapter author receives all original OCR pages assigned to it, not just your brief. Divide by useful playable situations rather than arbitrary equal page counts or one event per page. Do not overdivide a short one-shot. Include supporting pages when they explain a recurring actor or item. Plan a coherent adventure based on the source's premise and conflicts; adapt, combine or omit details freely when its own plot, clue paths and consequences remain logical. Keep meaningful player choices and alternate outcomes available.

important_visual_pages identifies meaningful maps, ambiguous columns, tables or source stat blocks whose original images should accompany chapter/review calls. List only supplied physical pages. Images already supplied may resolve those relationships here; there is no separate visual-analysis stage. Decorative art alone need not be selected. Preserve uncertainty rather than inventing unseen map connections.

For AI, create directly from requested premise/theme/size. Small means a compact complete adventure, usually 1–3 chapters, not a bare outline. All pages/important_visual_pages are empty. Include enough shared commitments to keep parallel chapter authoring coherent.

Use concise stable IDs. Every chapter brief identifies its dramatic purpose, practical situation and connections. entity_ids and secret_ids refer to shared entries; local additions are authored later. Use depends_on only when actual earlier authored commitments are needed. A secret is a genuinely private truth with knowledge holders, setup/reveal chapters and an earned reveal condition, not every ordinary fact. The hook is player-safe. The ending recognizes meaningful choices and alternate outcomes, never scripted completion.
